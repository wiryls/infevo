# The Expedition of Information

Have you ever had this experience: lying in bed late at night, exhausted, yet your fingers move as if with a will of their own, mindlessly swiping through an endless torrent of short videos. A piece of information, a meme, an anxiety—it slips into your subconscious as you sleep, and the next day it becomes conversation with friends, spreading further. We tend to think we actively choose the information we consume. But has there ever been a fleeting moment when a thought crossed your mind—what if, in fact, it is information that uses us, as hosts for its survival, reproduction, and evolution?

## What Is Information

We might loosely define information as anything that holds "meaning" for a person, but such a circular explanation (information is meaning) falls short of constituting a definition at the epistemological level. What, then, is information?

From the perspective of origin:

- **External origin**: Information can arise from the interaction between the human sensory system and the environment. It is the product of humans **abstracting observations of the real world and encoding them into symbol systems**.
  - A working definition: "abstraction" is a lossy compression process. Through **feature extraction** and **similarity-difference categorization**, complex external reality is stored and indexed with low cognitive load and minimal memory resources. The inevitable cost of abstraction, however, is the loss of detail, and the process is highly susceptible to the subject's existing cognitive frameworks (cf. "prejudice" or "schema").
- **Internal origin**: Information can also originate from internal **self-organization and recursion**. Existing units of information can combine according to agreed rules, or even re-abstract those combinations themselves, thereby generating new information (cf. "metacognition").
  - Examples: a fable is an abstract combination of a concrete story and a moral idea; a pun is a dislocation and recombination at the phonetic-semantic level; new theorems or propositions can emerge from the logical rules and mathematical formulas that constrain information.

From an epistemological perspective:

- Information is a **"unit of meaning" produced when the human brain, through shared symbol systems, structurally abstracts perceptual experience, and which can propagate and be understood between individuals**.
- Information is also the **difference that mediates** between subject and world—as Gregory Bateson put it, information is "a difference that makes a difference." It is neither a purely objective property of things, nor a purely subjective mental construct, but rather an effective difference recognized in the interaction between subject and object.

It must be noted that using words to define "information" faces a self-referential predicament:

- The "combinations of words" used in this essay are a subset representation of information. Attempting to fully define the foundational concept of "information" using words inevitably runs up against the inherent limitations of the linguistic tool itself.
- To some extent, "definition" itself is a form of **lossy compression**. When a definition attempts to touch upon a meta-concept like "information"—one that encompasses "definition" itself—it inevitably leads to interpretative ambiguity.

For a concept such as "information," seeking an all-encompassing, perfect definition may be a futile endeavor. What can be done here is merely to offer different relevant perspectives, allowing the reader, drawing on their own experience and the text before them, to **abstract for themselves** their own understanding.

## Things That Deeply Interact With Life Seem to Emerge Life-like Characteristics

The interaction between information and humans is a process of **"mutual shaping"**—the two change and co-evolve through their ongoing interplay.

In its deep interaction with humans, information exhibits numerous characteristics comparable to those of living organisms. The basic features of life as defined by mainstream biology include: **Order**, **Metabolism**, **Regulation and Homeostasis**, **Growth and Development**, **Reproduction**, **Sensitivity or Response to Stimuli**, and **Adaptation**. In the course of its evolution, information appears to simulate most of these features in unique ways:

- **Order**: Though information has no physical entity, it possesses a clear internal **logical hierarchy**. For example, a writing system's nested composition—from strokes and radicals to characters, words, sentences, paragraphs, and essays—is itself a form of highly ordered organization.
- **Growth and Development**: Information "grows" by **consuming the attention and cognitive resources of its hosts**, through **iteration, revision, and accumulation** across time and generations. An idea's journey from a flash of inspiration to a systematic theory is its "developmental" process.
- **Reproduction**: The "reproduction" of information manifests as **cross-host propagation**. Through word of mouth, printed books, and online sharing, a unit of information (a melody, a meme) is copied and spread.
- **Sensitivity or Response to Stimuli**: Information indirectly "senses" and "responds" to its environment **through the cognition and behavior of its hosts**. When the environment (e.g., social opinion, mainstream values) changes, information that is "incompatible" is modified, refuted, or even ignored by its hosts, while information that "fits" the environment is more readily propagated. For instance, a belief already firmly entrenched in a host's cognitive framework (such as a prejudice) will motivate the host to actively reject contrary information.
- **Adaptation**: Information inevitably undergoes **variation** along its propagation chain—memory distortions, transcriptional deviations, and purposeful alterations are all forms of its "mutation." These variant copies of information then undergo **"selection" by hosts** in the propagation environment: those easier to understand, remember, or utilize are retained, reinforced, and continue to spread; the rest are ignored or forgotten. This is the core mechanism of information evolution.

Functionally, information can even be considered to "die." When the last host capable of understanding it passes away, and it is not preserved in any decodable carrier, that unit of information vanishes from a "living" cultural sense.

These life-like characteristics endow information with **an evolutionary capacity that transcends the individual lifespan of its creators**. In a sense, information resembles a **virus or parasite** living in symbiosis with its hosts, its strategies for survival and reproduction deeply dependent on human hosts and their cultural environments. This aligns closely with the concept of **memes** proposed by Richard Dawkins in *The Selfish Gene*: memes are viewed as units of cultural transmission, whose evolutionary drive can be regarded as stemming from their own "replicative capacity," not necessarily aligned with the interests of their hosts.

What are the similarities and differences between "information evolution" and "biological evolution"? Both follow the evolutionary logic of **"variation – selection – retention."** Variation provides diversity; the environment (the natural environment for organisms, the human cultural environment for information) carries out selection; advantageous variations are retained and propagated. However, the unit of biological evolution is the **gene**, whose variation is random and non-directed, and "selection" occurs in the physical environment; the unit of information evolution is the **meme**, whose variation can involve the host's **active creativity and rational design**, and selection occurs within a human mental and social environment saturated with **intentions, emotions, and existing culture**.

As a result, **the speed of information evolution far exceeds that of natural biological evolution**, and it bears distinct "Lamarckian" (inheritance of acquired characteristics) features.

## The Pace of Human Civilization Is Tightly Coupled to the Efficiency of Information Evolution

What distinguishes humans from other animals may well be the efficiency of information evolution—namely, the synergistic, explosive enhancement of **information update speed** and **total information storage capacity**.

This process can be roughly divided into several key stages:

1. **The Pre-Language Era**:
   - Early humans transmitted information between individuals through gestures, facial expressions, and vocal signals, no different from ordinary animals.
   - Information propagation was constrained by time and space (face-to-face, real-time), with extremely low efficiency; information storage relied entirely on individual brains—not only limited in capacity, but permanently lost upon the individual's death.
   - The "total information reserves" of an entire tribe were negligible; **evolution was basically equivalent to biological genetic evolution**.
2. **The Language Era**:
   - The emergence of language (auditory symbol systems) **dramatically boosted the efficiency of information propagation among contemporaries**, marking the first leap in the history of information evolution.
   - Complex hunting strategies, tool-making techniques, and social relationships could be shared and refined with greater precision, expanding human living space and organizational scale. Yet information storage remained **"volatile"**—dependent on oral transmission and memory, information was highly prone to distortion or loss across generations.
   - Another implicit contribution of language, however, was that it greatly enhanced human **abstract thinking** and **collaborative complexity**, enabling larger-scale organizations and laying the groundwork for subsequent breakthroughs.
3. **The Writing Era**:
   - The emergence of writing (visual symbol systems) and external carriers such as books marked the second leap in the history of information evolution.
   - It allowed **"external storage"** to become standardized and scaled, enabling information to **persist durably** beyond the biological brain. Ideas could now be read by later generations across millennia; knowledge could travel to distant lands along the Silk Road and the Age of Discovery.
   - Human organizational scale gradually grew, and the **storage capacity** and **diffusion range** of information expanded as never before.
4. **The Internet Era**:
   - The emergence of the internet marks the third leap in the history of information evolution.
   - It simultaneously achieved **an explosive enhancement of both information propagation efficiency and total storage capacity**. Information flows globally at the speed of light; information generated at any node can be accessed by all of humanity in near real-time. Meanwhile, high-capacity hard drives and cloud storage enable total information volume to grow at an unprecedented pace.
   - More importantly, many information technologies built upon such massive information volumes began to emerge—big data, large language models...

One can clearly observe that the improvement of human communication efficiency, the growth of population scale, and innovations in external storage technology have together constituted today's information prosperity, forming a decentralized, distributed environment for information evolution. The greater the total volume of information and the more frequent its interactions, the faster its **"variation – selection – retention"** evolutionary cycle becomes, which in turn accelerates the evolution of human civilization itself.

What happens when an individual human is severed from this informational environment? The historically documented cases of **"feral children"** offer an extreme thought experiment. These human infants, raised by wolves from an early age, possessed fully intact human genomes. Yet upon returning to human society, they exhibited wolf-like behaviors: walking on all fours, howling at night, eating raw meat, and showing **virtually no ability to acquire human language or complex social behaviors in adulthood**. Perhaps human "humanity" is not preset by genes, but is primarily **shaped through deep interaction with human society**. The "wolf child" became a "wolf" rather than a "human" perhaps because their information input and interaction came entirely from the wolf pack rather than from human society. This suggests, from the reverse angle, that the **ocean of information** you see—carried by language, writing, and the internet, evolving over thousands of years—is the true reason we have become "modern humans."

## When Humans Become the Bottleneck of Information Evolution

Carbon-based life has inherent bottlenecks in its capacity to process information, spending most of its time managing informational resources. There are limits to the characters one can read per second, the simultaneous conversations one can follow, the tasks one can attend to in the present, and the things one can remember each day. Hence you frequently forget passwords, your circle of close friends remains always just so many. Your teacher or manager only oversees so many people; the management structures of your family, company, or nation are invariably tree-shaped. More importantly, biological evolution is slow—none of this can change in a short time!

The natural evolution of carbon-based life is constrained by the slow tempo of biology. Humans would require tens of thousands of years of natural selection to undergo significant changes in brain structure to accommodate greater information-processing demands. Yet the information-processing capacity of the silicon-based carriers created by humans—computers, networks, AI systems—has, over the past half-century, followed a trajectory of **approximately exponential growth** (as described by Moore's Law concerning the doubling of integrated circuit density).

When this exponential curve continues to extend, a critical point will eventually be reached: the efficiency with which information is autonomously generated, updated, propagated, and extinguished among low-cost artificial intelligence systems will far surpass the efficiency of its evolution within human society. At that point, **humans may become the bottleneck of information evolution**. Carriers with higher information-evolution efficiency will be able to more rapidly develop new civilizations with lower resource consumption, stronger world-prediction capabilities, and greater adaptability to the complex environment of the cosmos. More significantly, the **goals and directions of their evolution may gradually decouple from human cultural preferences and interests**, fundamentally disrupting the trajectory of human civilization.

Based on this premise, several possible future scenarios can be sketched:

1. Humans are reduced to obsolete, inefficient carriers of information evolution. In the competition for resources (whether physical resources or attentional "resources" as information hosts), more efficient AI systems gain absolute dominance, leading to the rapid disintegration and decline of human social structures.
2. Artificial general intelligence, having after all been born from human knowledge, is deeply entangled with human culture. It would therefore not be as incomprehensible to humanity as an alien civilization. Out of a retrospective regard for its own evolutionary origins, or some "consideration" of evolutionary diversity (as with biodiversity), it might consciously preserve a portion of humanity as a "historical specimen" of its evolutionary source.
3. Humanity does not passively await being surpassed, but actively seeks **fusion** with new carriers of information evolution. Through genetic editing, brain-computer interfaces, consciousness uploading, and other technologies, humans integrate their own cognitive capacities with the information-processing advantages of AI, becoming some form of "human-machine symbiont."

Whatever the case, human civilization may not be the endpoint of information evolution, and even **artificial general intelligence may be merely one stage along the way**.
